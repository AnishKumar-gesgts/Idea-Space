# Research Idea Criteria

A candidate must have a real scientific question, a specific claim beyond its closest current paper, a consequence that matters, and enough depth for a defensible publication. Search recent and foundational literature before asserting novelty. Explain what researchers would do differently if the result held.

The first test must be executable without new hardware or proprietary data, compare against the strongest fair baseline, use one physically compatible platform, include uncertainty and resource costs, and be capable of ending the idea. Software should answer the scientific question; connecting simulators or producing a parameter sweep is not itself the contribution.

For quantum-photonic work, connect the optical state or instrument to the actual downstream quantum-information task. Give an understandable chain from mechanism to observable to result. Do not claim hardware, native-architecture, or publication validation from a toy model.

Before proposing anything, check the [Strikeout List](Strikeout%20List.md) and the closest papers. A renamed version of a crossed-out idea is still crossed out.
